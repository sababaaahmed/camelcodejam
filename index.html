Quick fixes when you're stuck

  • Paste the error. Right-click your game, choose Inspect, open Console, copy the red error and paste it into BoodleBox with "fix this."
  • Ask for the full file. If you only got part of the code, ask: "Send the complete file."
  • Change one thing at a time and test after each change.
  • Save a backup. Fork your CodePen before any big change.
  • Describe what you see. "The ghost moves but never disappears when I catch it" gets better help than "it's broken."

Step 1: Put your game online (pick your path)

Most teams

CodePen

  1. Ask BoodleBox for "the complete game in one HTML file."
  2. At codepen.io, click Create, then Pen.
  3. Paste everything into the HTML box.
  4. Click Save and copy the URL.
  5. Tip: change /pen/ to /full/ in the link for full-screen play.
Also easy

p5.js Web Editor

  1. Sign in at editor.p5js.org.
  2. Paste your sketch and press Play to test it.
  3. File, then Save.
  4. File, then Share, and copy the Fullscreen link.
Godot or pygame

itch.io

  1. Export for Web (Godot) or build with pygbag (pygame).
  2. Zip the folder that contains index.html.
  3. On itch.io, create a new project, set Kind to HTML, and upload the zip.
  4. Check "This file will be played in the browser." For Godot 4, also turn on SharedArrayBuffer support, or use the single-threaded export.
  5. Set visibility to Public (or Restricted and share the link) and copy the URL.

Step 2: Put your studio website online

  1. In Google Stitch, open your design and export the code (HTML).
  2. Paste it into a new CodePen, save, and copy the link.
  3. Add a "Play now" button on your site that links to your game.
  4. If the export doesn't look right, bring it to the Saturday tech consultation. As a backup, share your Stitch project link plus screenshots.

Step 3: Final check before you submit

  • Both links open in a private or incognito window without logging in.
  • The game starts and is playable from beginning to end.
  • Instructions are on the start screen or in your description.
  • Your website links to your game.
  • You've saved your best prompts somewhere to paste in.
  • Everyone on your team agrees the project is done.

Rules

  • All game and website work starts after the sub-theme reveal on Friday at 7:30 PM.
  • AI tools are allowed and encouraged.
  • Game engines like Godot or pygame are allowed, as long as the game plays in a web browser from a link.
  • Teams of 2–3, or solo. Everyone must be a registered Conn student.
  • Use only art, sounds and fonts you made, generated, or have permission to use, and credit them.
  • Keep content appropriate for a campus audience.

FAQ

I've never coded. Can I still join?

Yes! The event is built for beginners. Friday's workshops teach you everything, and there's a tech consultation on Saturday. There's even a Best Beginner Team award.

Can I use Godot, pygame or another engine?

Yes, as long as the game runs in a web browser from a link. Export for the web and upload to itch.io. See the itch.io path under How to submit.

I don't have a team. What should I do?

Come anyway! You can go solo, or find teammates during team formation on Friday at 7:30 PM.

Do I need my own laptop?

No. Computers are available in the PC Classroom. Save your work to your CodePen or Stitch account, not the lab computer.

Do I have to be there the whole time?

No. Come for Friday's workshops, then work whenever suits you. Just submit by Sunday at 10:00 AM. We'd love to see you at Sunday's dinner and ceremony.

Can I start building before Friday?

You can brainstorm and practice with the tools, but all project work has to start after the sub-theme reveal at 7:30 PM Friday.

What if my link breaks after I submit?

You can edit your form response until 10:00 AM. After that, contact an organizer right away.
